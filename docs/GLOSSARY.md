# Glossary

Plain-language terms you will see in this project, in PubMed papers, and in ECD clinic conversations.

This is **not medical advice**. Meanings are simplified for families and contributors. Confirm details with an ECD specialist.

---

## Diseases and cells

**Histiocyte**  
An immune cell that normally helps fight infection and clean up damaged tissue. In ECD and LCH, these cells behave abnormally and can build up in organs.

**Histiocytosis / histiocytic neoplasm**  
A group of rare diseases of histiocytes. Doctors often treat them with oncology-style tools (molecular tests, targeted drugs), even though they are not the same as typical leukemia or lymphoma.

**ECD (Erdheim-Chester disease)**  
A rare histiocytic disease that usually affects adults. Common sites include bone, kidneys/retroperitoneum, heart/aorta, and sometimes the brain and pituitary.

**LCH (Langerhans cell histiocytosis)**  
A related histiocytic disease, more often diagnosed in children but also seen in adults. It can overlap with ECD in some people.

**Mixed histiocytosis**  
A person (or biopsy) showing features of more than one histiocytosis, often LCH plus ECD. Papers may label this mixed, overlapping, or “LCH/ECD.”

**RDD (Rosai-Dorfman disease)**  
Another histiocytic disorder. It sometimes appears with ECD (mixed ECD/RDD) in case reports.

**CNS (central nervous system)**  
The brain and spinal cord. “CNS-ECD” means disease involving those structures (for example brainstem, meninges, or pituitary).

**Meninges / meningitis / pachymeningitis / leptomeningitis**  
The membranes covering the brain. Inflammation or infiltration of those membranes can cause neurologic symptoms. “Monocytic meningitis” means extra monocytes (a type of white cell) in the spinal fluid.

## Genes, mutations, and tests

**Mutation**  
A DNA change in disease cells. In ECD it is often in the MAPK pathway. The mutation is usually in the histiocytes, not something you “caught.”

**MAPK pathway**  
A cellular “on switch” for growth signals. BRAF, MEK (MAP2K1), KRAS, NRAS, and ARAF sit on this pathway. Many ECD/LCH treatments try to turn this switch down.

**BRAF / BRAF V600E**  
A gene commonly mutated in ECD. **V600E** is a specific, well-studied change. Not every ECD patient has it.

**Wild-type BRAF**  
Testing did **not** find a BRAF V600E (or similar) mutation. Other MAPK mutations (for example MAP2K1) may still be present.

**MAP2K1 (MEK1)**  
Another MAPK gene. Mutations here are a reason doctors may use a **MEK inhibitor** even when BRAF is negative.

**NGS (next-generation sequencing)**  
A lab method that reads many genes at once from tissue or sometimes blood, looking for targetable mutations.

**IHC (immunohistochemistry)**  
Staining tissue on a slide to see proteins (for example BRAF V600E protein) under a microscope.

**Liquid biopsy / CSF liquid biopsy**  
Testing blood or spinal fluid (CSF) for mutation fragments instead of (or in addition to) a solid-tissue biopsy.

**PMID**  
PubMed ID — the unique number for a paper in PubMed. Example: [41562816](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41562816/).

**PMC / PMCID**  
PubMed Central — a library of **open-access** full texts. This agent can fetch PMC XML when a paper is freely available; it cannot legally scrape paywalled PDFs.

**DOI**  
Digital Object Identifier — a stable web ID for a paper (starts with `10.`).

**NCT number**  
ClinicalTrials.gov study ID (starts with `NCT`).

## Treatments you will see in papers (and on the bottle)

Research papers almost always use the **generic** (scientific) name. Prescriptions, pharmacy labels, and family conversations usually use the **brand** name. Same medicine — two names.

Listing a drug here is **not** a recommendation. Brand names below are common U.S. names; other countries may use different brands. Whether a drug is approved for ECD specifically is a question for the care team (for example, **Zelboraf** has an FDA indication for BRAF V600-mutant ECD; others may be used off-label or under other labeled uses).

**Targeted therapy**  
A drug aimed at a molecular pathway (here, usually MAPK), rather than traditional broad chemotherapy alone.

**BRAF inhibitor**  
Turns down an overactive BRAF signal. Papers: vemurafenib, dabrafenib. Bottles: Zelboraf, Tafinlar.

**MEK inhibitor**  
Turns down MEK, a step after BRAF on the same pathway. Papers: cobimetinib, trametinib, binimetinib. Bottles: Cotellic, Mekinist, Mektovi. Used with a BRAF inhibitor in some BRAF-mutant disease, or alone in some BRAF-negative MAPK-mutant disease.

**Dab/Tra (dabrafenib + trametinib)**  
The combination **Tafinlar + Mekinist**. Common in papers on mixed LCH/ECD and other MAPK-driven histiocytosis.

**Zelboraf + Cotellic**  
The combination **vemurafenib + cobimetinib**. You may see this pairing in melanoma literature; ECD papers more often discuss Zelboraf alone or other MAPK combinations — check the specific paper.

**Cladribine**  
A chemotherapy-type drug used in some histiocytosis cases, including CNS disease, in published reports. Confirm the exact product name with the treating team; “cladribine” is not always the same pill as Mavenclad.

**Interferon-alpha**  
An older systemic therapy still mentioned in historical ECD papers. Brand names vary.

**Steroids (corticosteroids)**  
Anti-inflammatory drugs. Papers often note they help little or only briefly in ECD.

## Timing and outcomes (case tables)

**Sx → Dx (symptoms to diagnosis)**  
How long from first relevant symptoms until the disease was named. Long intervals are common in ECD because it is rare and can mimic other conditions.

**Dx → Rx (diagnosis to treatment)**  
How long from diagnosis until a described treatment (often the targeted therapy of interest) was started.

**Early vs delayed therapy**  
In this project’s case tables, these labels mean **when targeted therapy started relative to symptoms or diagnosis**, as the paper describes — **not** how fast the patient felt better.

- Rapid improvement after a late start is still **delayed therapy**.
- Starting soon after diagnosis can be labeled **early** even if symptoms had lasted years before anyone made the diagnosis.

**Neurologic outcome**  
What happened to brain/nerve symptoms or MRI after treatment, **as the paper reported** (improved, stable, progressed, mixed). Scores such as **SARA** and **ICARS** measure ataxia (unsteady movement).

**Abstract-limited**  
The extractor only saw the PubMed abstract, not the full article. Timing and outcomes are often missing until full text (PMC) is used.

## How papers are classified

**Case report**  
Usually one patient. Very useful in rare disease; **cannot** prove what works for everyone.

**Case series**  
A handful of patients from one center or paper.

**Cohort / retrospective review**  
Many patients looked back over time. Stronger than a single case, still not a randomized trial.

**Scoping / systematic review**  
A paper that **summarizes other papers**. Counts in those reviews are not the same as extracting each original case.

**Clinical trial**  
A prospective study of an intervention. Status (recruiting, completed, etc.) should come from ClinicalTrials.gov, not guesses.

**Consensus recommendations / guidelines**  
Expert agreement (for example the 2020 *Blood* ECD paper). Helpful orientation; still not individualized care.

## Terms this software uses

**Evidence record**  
One atomic claim tied to one source (PMID, quote, study type). The system discards claims it cannot ground in the supplied text.

**Case record**  
Structured fields about a published patient or small series: disease label, mutation, therapies, timing, outcomes, plus a supporting quote.

**Provenance / validation**  
A fail-closed check: the quote must appear in the title/abstract (and full text when used). Paraphrases are rejected.

**Critic**  
A second pass that flags over-claiming (for example treating one case as proof for all patients).

**Aggregation**  
Counting and tabulating **only what records contain**. Missing stays missing; the tool does not invent statistics.

**Research aid**  
Output is for reading papers and preparing questions. It is not a diagnosis or a treatment plan.

## Imaging (brief)

**MRI**  
Detailed pictures of brain and soft tissue; often used to follow CNS lesions.

**PET / PET-CT**  
Scan that shows metabolically active disease in bone and other organs; used in ECD staging and follow-up.

**Deauville score**  
A 1–5 scale sometimes used to grade PET activity (higher = more uptake).

## Reading a case-corpus table

When you see a row:

1. Open the **PMID** link and read at least the abstract.
2. Treat **n = 1** as one person’s story.
3. Treat **n = 13** or **n = 32** as a **review or series**, not one patient.
4. If timing is `not_reported` or `unclear`, do not fill in the blank from memory.
5. Bring disagreements and gaps to the specialist — that is the intended use.
